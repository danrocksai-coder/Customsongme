What to Include in Your Story for a Custom Anniversary Song

By Dan Rock, Dan the Song Man · Updated October 2026

The best anniversary songs are built from small, real details, not big general statements. You do not need to be a writer. Here is what to gather before you order, so the song sounds like the two of you.

The basics

  • Both first names, and any nicknames you use for each other.
  • How many years you are celebrating, and the date if it matters.
  • Who the song is for, and who will hear it. A surprise at dinner and a song for the whole family are different songs.

Your story, in a few moments

Pick three to five moments instead of trying to tell everything. For each one, a sentence or two is enough.

  • How you met and what you each thought of the other.
  • A place that belongs to you: the first apartment, a restaurant, a beach, a porch.
  • A hard stretch you got through together, if you want it in the song.
  • A tradition you still keep, like a Sunday ritual or an annual trip.
  • A small thing only the two of you would know, like a phrase, a song on the radio or a running joke.

What they are like

Add a few lines about the person receiving the song: what makes them laugh, what they always say, what you notice about them that nobody else does. These details are what make the lyrics feel like them and not like any couple.

The feeling you want

Tell me whether you want a tender ballad, something upbeat and playful, a country feel or something else. If you are unsure, describe how you want them to feel when they hear it, and I will pick the style.

What to leave out

  • Anything you would not want played in front of family or friends.
  • Painful events you would rather not revisit. Say so, and I will keep them out.
  • Long lists of names. A few key people are better than everyone.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to write for a custom anniversary song?

A few short paragraphs is plenty. Names, how you met, a few specific memories and one or two inside jokes give a songwriter more to work with than a long, general description.

Do I need to be a good writer?

No. Bullet points, fragments and voice-note style rambling all work. What matters is the specific details, not the wording.

What if we have been together for decades?

Pick a handful of moments instead of trying to cover everything: the first apartment, a trip, a hard year you got through, a tradition you still keep. A few real moments make a stronger song than a full timeline.
